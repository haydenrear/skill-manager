WHAT THIS SETTLES, and it cuts three ways.

1. GOAL-one-home-one-answer clause 1 FAILS in general (DEF-115) and HOLDS on the
   operator's root home. Every finding here is the WRAPPER/CONTENT shape --
   FOREIGN_PATH_IN_SHIM -- which is exactly the shape HIS-21 taught `home verify`
   to read and where the two readers now agree. NOT ONE is the SYMLINK shape
   DEF-115 is about. So the failing clause has no known instance in the home the
   goal was written about. That narrows the FAIL; it does not remove it, because
   a clone that links into a parent store is the normal case and is precisely
   where a naive fix would start reporting false positives.

2. All five are REPAIRABLE -- every one prints `repair: rewrite that path to
   <this home>/...`. So DEF-112's non-converging FOREIGN_PATH_IN_SHIM (a finding
   HomeFixpointLaw could not clear) still has NO known real instance.

3. THE LEAK POINTS THE WRONG WAY, and that is the finding worth carrying.
   The ROOT home's shims execute code inside the PROJECT home. Not the reverse.
   The tier that is supposed to be the parent of everything is, for three of its
   tools, a client of a repository checkout that can be deleted, rebased, or
   have its home rebuilt by any ticket agent. This is the epic's founding defect
   in its most consequential direction, alive, on the machine, at the tip.
